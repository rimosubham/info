— The constraints

Four constraints that made this the hardest brief we'd ever worked on.

Every other engagement gives you tools. This one took them away. Each constraint forced new tactics that didn't exist in our previous playbook.

— Constraint 01

No link exchanges allowed

Our biggest tactic across every prior engagement was off the table. Canva had a strict policy: no reciprocal link arrangements, no ABC swaps, no quid pro quo. Every link had to be earned without offering anything in return.

— Constraint 02

A brand-new domain

canva.in launched with zero authority. While canva.com had a DR in the high 90s, the new TLD started from absolute scratch. Authority had to be built, not transferred — while users globally still defaulted to .com.

— Constraint 03

12+ regional languages

India isn't a single market. After English, Hindi is widely spoken — but the country runs on Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia, and others. Outreach had to span all of them.

— Constraint 04

Authority signals to Google

For Google to treat canva.in as the authoritative India destination — and not a low-trust regional clone — the link profile had to look like a credible India-native brand from day one. No spam, no shortcuts, no offshore link farms.

— The strategy

Four playbooks we had to invent from scratch.

When you can't use exchanges and the domain has zero authority, every comfortable tactic disappears. Here are the four new playbooks we developed — running in parallel for 12 months.

Unconventional
i.

Telephonic outreach to local magazines

Most link builders email. The Indian regional press doesn't always respond to email — especially in tier-2 and tier-3 cities. So we picked up the phone.

  • Direct calls to editorial desks — reached out to regional magazines and news sites by phone, in the local language where possible.
  • Trust-building before pitch — first calls were never about a link. They were about "we're launching an India experience, we'd love your feedback."
  • Why it worked — regional editors get pitched 50 emails a day from spammers. A phone call from a real human stood out. Conversion rates were 5-10× email outreach.
  • Slow but compounding — each successful call seeded a relationship. The same editors came back with follow-up coverage as canva.in grew.
No exchange = creative
ii.

Brand mention plays instead of links

Without link exchanges, every link had to be a one-way gift from the publisher. So we shifted the ask: not "link to us," but "mention us when relevant." Brand mentions on authority sites still drive trust, citations, and organic discovery — even without a hyperlink.

  • Direct outreach to authors/content writers — bypassed editorial inboxes and went to writers directly.
  • Mention-first, link-second — a brand mention without a link still elevates the article's relevance to AI crawlers and Google's entity graph.
  • Lower barrier, higher acceptance — writers who said no to "add a link" said yes to "mention us in this list of relevant tools."
  • Long-tail effect — mentions on authority sites like Forbes, BigCommerce, and DigitalOcean compound — AI tools start citing the article, our brand gets cited too.
Strategic
iii.

Competitor reverse-engineering

Canva has plenty of well-funded competitors with strong India presence: design tools, photo editors, AI image generators. We mined every one of their backlink profiles for the specifically India-relevant placements.

  • Backlink profile audits — systematically pulled every competitor's backlinks via Ahrefs, filtered for India-based publishers.
  • Topical fit analysis — categorised each placement by intent: tutorials, comparisons, listicles, "best of" guides.
  • Identical-fit pitching — for every page where a competitor was listed, evaluated whether canva.in could be added.
  • The result — caught up to and surpassed several competitors on India-specific backlinks within 9 months.
Geo-specific
iv.

Multilingual regional push

For canva.in to be seen as truly India-native, the link profile needed regional language coverage — not just English. This meant outreach in Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, and other major scripts.

  • Regional blog mapping — built a database of 500+ regional language blogs across 10+ Indian languages.
  • Native-language pitching — outreach in the publisher's own language wherever possible. Native-language pitches converted 3× English ones.
  • Topical relevance focus — pitched regional design, education, and creator-focused publications.
  • Authority signal to Google — a multilingual backlink profile told Google: this is a legitimate India brand, not an offshore clone.
— The quality bar

15+ metrics every link had to pass.

Canva's reputation is global. One spammy link could damage years of trust. So we built a 15+ point quality checklist that every potential placement had to clear before outreach.

✓ Link in first half of article
✓ Less than 5 external links on page
✓ Page traffic > 500/month
✓ Brand mention required, not just URL
✓ Topical relevance to design or creativity
✓ Site Domain Rating > 30
✓ Page indexed in Google
✓ No suspicious anchor text patterns
✓ Page age > 6 months
✓ Site has organic traffic from target geo
✓ Author has verifiable writing history
✓ No casino, PBN, or spam neighbours
✓ Natural anchor (not keyword-stuffed)
✓ Content fits naturally, not as obvious sponsorship
✓ Editorial guidelines respected, no payment required

The motive was simple: one strong link should outrank thirty normal ones.

From a zero-authority domain to one of India's most-cited design platforms.

Months 1–3

Q2 2025
