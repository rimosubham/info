— What this actually is

If Google was a school, links are the gold stars.

The more credible classmates that vouch for you, the more the teacher trusts your work. Backlinks are exactly that: every reputable website that links to yours is one more vote saying "this is the real deal."

Most of the internet runs on this mechanic. Your competitors are working it every day. The question isn't whether you need link building. The question is whether the links you're getting are helping or quietly hurting you.

— A simple test

"Would I email this site if I didn't need a link?"

If the answer is no, the link probably isn't worth having. Most spam links fail this test instantly. Every link we build passes it.

— The rejection list

What we
refuse to do.

Half of link building is knowing when to walk away. These four tactics still make agencies money. We won't touch any of them.

PBNs

Private Blog Networks. Otherwise known as "fake blog farms with extra steps."

A PBN is a network of sites built only to link to clients. Google has been killing them since 2014. We don't own any. We don't rent any. We don't link to any.

Link Farms & Wheels

Sites that link to anyone with a pulse. And $20.

Reciprocal link wheels, ABC swaps, mass directory submissions. They pump up your numbers for two months, then Google catches up and your rankings vaporise.

Paid Editorial Placements

"Sponsored content" without the sponsored disclosure.

Buying a link disguised as an article violates Google's guidelines. The fact that half the agency industry does it doesn't make it okay. It just means half the industry is a few algorithm updates from collapse.

Comment & Forum Spam

"Great post! Visit my-site.com" — at scale.

Auto-posted comments, spammy forum signatures, profile-link farming. Google ignored these in 2010. We've never been tempted to start now.
