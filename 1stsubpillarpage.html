Best Time to Visit Darjeeling: A Month-by-Month Honest Guide

Every month has its magic and its trade-offs. Here's exactly what you'll see, feel, pay and put up with from January to December - so you pick the right month for the right reasons.

Darjeeling has four distinct personalities a year. Spring brings rhododendrons and bloom. Summer brings school holidays and crowds. Monsoon brings green hills and landslides. Autumn-winter brings the clearest Mount Kanchenjunga mornings you'll ever see. Pick the wrong month and you'll spend three days staring at fog through a hotel window. Pick the right one and you'll understand exactly why the British called this place the Queen of the Hills. Below is everything you actually need to know - month by month, with the good, the not-so-good and the smart-saver tip for each.

01 · The overview

Darjeeling seasons at a glance

Darjeeling sits at 6,710 ft and has a sub-tropical highland climate. The year breaks cleanly into four phases. Use this as your speed-read before scrolling to the detailed month entries below.

Mar - May

Jun - Sep

02 · The deep dive

Darjeeling month by month - all 12 months

The honest breakdown. What's good, what's not, what it'll cost you and what your trip will actually look like.

January

January is the coldest month in Darjeeling. Days are crisp and sunny but nights can dip close to zero. The first 10 days of the month see leftover New Year traffic, but from January 10 onwards the town empties out beautifully. Mornings have a gentle mist that burns off by 9 AM. Snow at Tiger Hill and Sandakphu is possible 2-4 times during the month.

What's beautiful
  • Crystal-clear Kanchenjunga afternoons
  • 40% lower hotel rates (after Jan 10)
  • Almost zero tourist crowd
  • Highest snow probability of the year
  • Cosy fireplaces at heritage hotels
Problems you'll face
  • Nights drop to 1-3°C - serious cold
  • Some homestays in offbeat villages shut
  • Sandakphu trek often closed mid-month
  • Early sunset at 5:00 PM cuts daylight
  • No major festivals in January
Smart saver
Book Jan 10-25 window for the lowest rates of the year on heritage stays (Windamere, Mayfair drop 25-30%). Skip the New Year week unless you specifically want the party vibe.

February is still cold but slightly warmer than January, and the daylight stretches longer. This is the quiet sweet spot before peak spring rush. Losar (Tibetan New Year) often falls in February or early March - if you time it right, you'll catch the monasteries in full prayer-flag-flying celebration. The town starts coming alive towards the last week.

March is when Darjeeling wakes up. Rhododendrons start to bloom, magnolias open, and the first flush of Darjeeling tea is harvested - the most prized tea of the year. Days are warm in the sun and cool in the shade. Skies are mostly clear. This is also when Holi falls, celebrated locally with restraint compared to North India.

  • Holi week can be busy
  • Cool early mornings still need woollens
Smart saver
Visit Happy Valley Tea Estate in the second week of March - you can buy the year's first-flush tea straight from production at factory rates.

April is when Darjeeling looks like a postcard. The hills are emerald green, rhododendrons are at full bloom, and Singalila National Park turns into a corridor of pink and red. Day temperatures are perfect for sightseeing, and the night chill is gentle. The downside - everyone else has the same idea. Hotels need to be booked 4-6 weeks ahead.

What's beautiful
  • Full rhododendron bloom at Singalila
  • Pleasant 18°C afternoons - light jacket weather
  • Best photography conditions
  • All offbeat homestays open
  • Sandakphu trek season opens
Problems you'll face

  • Tiger Hill gets crowded - reach by 4:00 AM
  • Mall Road packed in the evenings
  • Toy train fully booked weeks ahead
  • Occasional sudden hailstorms
Smart saver
Book April 1-10 instead of mid-April. Prices haven't peaked yet, and the bloom has already started. You save 15-20% and dodge the school holiday rush that begins April 15.

May is the warmest month in Darjeeling and that's exactly why so many travellers come - to escape the 40°C heat down in the plains. Day temperatures sit comfortably at 18-20°C. This is school summer vacation season across India, which means Darjeeling is packed. Buddha Purnima celebrations light up local monasteries. Brief afternoon showers begin from the last week.

What's beautiful
  • Warmest, most pleasant weather of the year
  • Buddha Purnima at Ghoom & Bhutia Busty
  • Family-friendly conditions
  • Mountain wildflower season
  • Tea tastings - second flush available
Problems you'll face
  • Peak season prices - hotels 50-70% above winter
  • Mall Road feels like Delhi at peak hour
  • 2-hour wait times at Tiger Hill
  • Pre-monsoon haze obscures Kanchenjunga from last week
  • Restaurants run out of food early evening
Smart saver
If you're a family forced to travel in May, pick a homestay in Lamahatta or Lepchajagat instead of staying in town. Same temperature, 60% lower price, zero crowd.

June is a month of two halves. The first two weeks ride the May momentum - school holidays still on, prices high, crowds thick. From around June 15, the monsoon arrives. Rains intensify, mist takes over, and the tourist rush evaporates. Prices crash. If you don't mind a wet trip, late June can be very atmospheric - lush, quiet and cheap.

What's beautiful
  • Lush green hills after first rains
  • 40% price drop after June 15
  • Empty cafes and Mall Road
  • Waterfalls at peak flow
Problems you'll face
  • Kanchenjunga hidden behind clouds for days
  • Landslide alerts on Hill Cart Road
  • Toy train cancellations begin
  • Slippery monastery floors and steps
  • Sandakphu trek closed by mid-month
Smart saver
Late June is when heritage hotels run "monsoon getaway" packages - Mayfair, Cedar Inn and Windamere offer 35-45% discounts. Just don't bank on clear mountain views.

July is monsoon at full power. Rainfall peaks, mist becomes near-permanent, and Kanchenjunga essentially disappears from the sky. The Hill Cart Road sees frequent landslides, the toy train runs only when conditions permit, and a "quick day trip" can turn into a stranded overnight if a slide hits. Unless you have an unmovable date and absolutely don't care about views, skip July.

What's beautiful
  • Misty, moody, photographers' frame-worthy
  • Cheapest hotel rates of the year
  • You'll have Mall Road almost to yourself
  • Rains feed dramatic mountain waterfalls
Problems you'll face
  • Landslide risk on access roads
  • Toy train often cancelled with no refund
  • Zero Kanchenjunga visibility most days
  • Leeches in Singalila / forest areas
  • Damp clothing and slow drying
  • Tiger Hill mostly pointless
Smart saver
If you absolutely must travel in July, fly to Bagdogra and use private cabs only - never shared SUVs - and build a 1-day buffer into your return ticket. Travel insurance is non-negotiable.

August continues the July monsoon pattern but slightly less intense. Rains taper off in the third week. Independence Day brings a handful of local visitors, but otherwise the town remains quiet. If you're a monsoon traveller, late August is when you should plan - the colour palette is at its most vivid.

What's beautiful

September is the underrated month. Monsoon clouds break up week by week, hills are at their greenest, and by the third week the first crisp autumn mornings appear. Tourist crowds haven't returned yet, so prices remain off-season. If you don't mind a few showers, late September is a quiet, beautiful window with the freshness of monsoon and the gradually returning views of autumn.

What's beautiful

  • Off-season prices on heritage hotels

  • Damp early mornings
  • Leech presence in forest trails
Smart saver
Last week of September is the best value-for-money window. You get near-clear skies, lush greenery, low crowds, and 25-35% off hotel rates. Book before festival-rush kicks in mid-October.

October is, hands down, the best month to visit Darjeeling. Post-monsoon skies are at their cleanest. Kanchenjunga reveals itself from Tiger Hill, Observatory Hill, even from Mall Road in the early afternoons. Temperatures are crisp - jacket weather in the morning, t-shirt weather by noon. Durga Puja and Diwali fall in this window, bringing local festivals and Bengali tourist rush.

What's beautiful
  • Clearest Kanchenjunga views all year
  • Perfect daytime temperatures
  • Durga Puja and Diwali festivities
  • Local Hill Cart Road well-maintained again
  • All offbeat villages fully accessible
Problems you'll face
  • Durga Puja week (varies by year) is the worst crowd surge of the year
  • Hotel rates spike 60-80% during Puja week
  • Tiger Hill packed - go before 4:30 AM
  • Restaurant wait times can hit 45 minutes
  • Toy train booked weeks ahead
Smart saver
Avoid Durga Puja week - check the dates for your year. The first 10 days of October and the last week of October (post-Diwali) have the same gorgeous weather at 40% lower rates and far fewer crowds.

November is October without the Puja-week chaos. Skies stay clear, mornings turn crisper, and the air takes on that distinctive Himalayan winter sharpness. This is the photographer's month - long golden hours, low haze, snow-tipped peaks. Tea estate tours are perfect, the toy train runs reliably, and offbeat villages like Lamahatta and Tinchuley are at their cleanest.

What's beautiful
  • Cleanest mountain visibility of the year
  • Less crowded than October post-Puja
  • Long golden-hour sunsets
  • Perfect trekking conditions in Singalila
  • Honeymooners and couples-focused
Problems you'll face
  • Hotel rates still peak-season
  • Nights drop to 6-8°C - heavy woollens needed
  • Pre-Christmas booking rush starts last week
  • Some homestays in offbeat villages start winter shut-down
Smart saver
Mid-November (Nov 10-25) is the smartest booking window of the entire year. Cleaner views than April-May, fewer crowds than October, and rates 20% below the peak of December's Christmas week.

Mid (early) / Peak (late)

December is a tale of three weeks. The first two weeks are quiet, cold and stunningly clear - one of the best weather windows of the entire year. From around December 18 onwards, the Christmas-New Year rush begins. The town fills with families, prices double, and Tiger Hill becomes a stampede. By December 31, every heritage hotel is booked and Mall Road is wall-to-wall. Snow on Tiger Hill and Sandakphu becomes possible from the third week.

What's beautiful
  • Possible snow at Tiger Hill and Sandakphu
  • Christmas decorations across Mall Road and Glenary's
  • Bonfire dinners at heritage hotels
  • Crystal-clear afternoon views
  • First two weeks offer real value
Problems you'll face
  • Holiday-week prices 80-100% above standard
  • Tiger Hill traffic jams from Dec 25 onwards
  • Restaurants fully booked from Christmas Eve
  • Sub-zero nights at higher altitudes
  • Most homestays in offbeat villages closed
Smart saver
December 1-15 is one of the best secret windows. Same crystal weather as November, possible early snow, and 25-30% lower rates than the Christmas crush. Book by mid-October - this window fills fast once word gets out.

03 · The match

Pick your month by travel style

Different travellers want different things from Darjeeling. Here's the best-month-by-priority breakdown.

✿
Honeymoon

Mid-February or mid-November

February for off-peak prices and Valentine's privacy. November for clear views, golden sunsets and post-monsoon greenery without summer crowds.

☼
Family with kids

Late March or early October

Pleasant 12-18°C, no heavy rain or extreme cold. School holidays in May get too crowded. April second week still works if you book 6+ weeks ahead.

❄
Snow chasers

Last week of December to mid-February

The only window when snow is genuinely possible at Tiger Hill and Sandakphu. Book a Sandakphu Land Rover ride for the best chance.

☀
Kanchenjunga views

October to November and mid-March to mid-April

The five highest-clarity weeks of the year. October-November after monsoon, and pre-summer March-April before haze sets in.

☯
Budget traveller

Mid-January or last week of September

Lowest hotel rates of the year (30-40% off). Mid-January is quietest, late-September gives near-peak weather at off-season prices.

⚙
Photographer

November and March

Cleanest mountain visibility, long golden hours, dramatic light, no haze. November for autumn warmth, March for spring rhododendrons.

✂
Solo traveller

Late September or mid-November

Quiet hostels, no festival crowds, plenty of cafe space to read and write, locals have time to chat.

⛰
Trekker (Sandakphu)

Mid-October to mid-November, mid-March to mid-April

Singalila trekking permits issued only in these windows. Best clarity, manageable temperatures, no monsoon risk on trails.

05 · The packing

What to pack by season

Darjeeling weather can swing 10°C between sunrise and noon. Pack for layering, not for a single temperature.

Spring / Summer (Mar - May)

  • Light cotton clothes for the day
  • Light woollen jacket / sweater for evenings
  • Comfortable walking shoes with grip
  • Sunglasses + sunscreen (UV is sharp at altitude)
  • Cap or hat for Tiger Hill
  • Light umbrella for late-May showers

Monsoon (Jun - Sep)

  • Waterproof jacket with hood
  • Quick-dry pants
  • Waterproof trekking shoes
  • Dry bag for electronics
  • Insect repellent (for leeches)
  • Spare set of dry clothes

Autumn (Oct - Nov)

  • Light woollens for daytime
  • Heavy fleece / down jacket for early mornings
  • Thermals for Tiger Hill 4 AM
  • Gloves & a beanie
  • Power bank (cold drains batteries)
  • Sturdy shoes with grip

Winter (Dec - Feb)

  • Heavy down jacket
  • Thermals (top and bottom)
  • Woollen socks (multiple pairs)
  • Insulated waterproof boots
  • Gloves, beanie, scarf
  • Lip balm + moisturiser (cold dries skin fast)
  • Hand warmers for Tiger Hill

07 · The questions

Frequently asked questions

Which is the best month to visit Darjeeling?
The best months are October, November, March, April and May. October-November gives the clearest Kanchenjunga views and crisp post-monsoon skies. March-May brings rhododendron blooms and pleasant 12-20°C daytime weather. For our money, November is the sweet spot - same gorgeous weather as October, fewer crowds after Durga Puja, and prices about 20% lower than the December Christmas peak.
Which month is best for snowfall in Darjeeling?
Snowfall is rare inside Darjeeling town itself but very possible at higher altitudes like Sandakphu (11,930 ft), Tiger Hill, and surrounding ridges. The highest snow probability is from the third week of December through the second week of February. January has the most consistent chances. Booking a Sandakphu Land Rover ride during this window dramatically increases your odds of seeing snow.
What is the off-season in Darjeeling?
The official off-season is July to September (monsoon), when hotel rates drop 30-40% and tourist crowds thin out, but landslide risk and washed-out toy train services make it tough. January (after Jan 10) and early February are also lower-traffic with discounted stays - and much better weather than monsoon. If your priority is value over crowd-free, late September is the smartest off-season window.
Which is the coldest month in Darjeeling?
January is the coldest month with daytime temperatures of 5-9°C and nights dropping to 1-4°C. February follows closely. December nights also dip below 5°C. If you don't handle cold well, pick March or October instead - you still get great views without sub-zero mornings.
Is December a good time to visit Darjeeling?
December is excellent if you want Christmas-New Year festivities, possible snowfall and the cosiest hill-station vibe. The downsides are very cold nights, peak holiday-week pricing (Dec 24 - Jan 2) and crowded Tiger Hill mornings in the last 10 days. December 1-15 is the smart-money window - same weather as the holiday peak at 25-30% lower rates.
Can I see Mount Everest from Darjeeling and which month is best for it?
On extremely clear mornings between November and February, Mount Everest appears as a tiny peak on the far horizon to the west of Kanchenjunga from Tiger Hill. Mid-October to mid-November and the first two weeks of March give the highest success rate. Most days only Kanchenjunga and the closer Eastern Himalayan peaks are visible.
Is Darjeeling safe during monsoon?
Monsoon Darjeeling (June-September) has high landslide risk on NH-55 and the Hill Cart Road. The toy train is often cancelled, Kanchenjunga stays hidden and roads can shut down for hours. We don't recommend it unless you're a confirmed off-season traveller wanting lush green hills and 40-50% lower hotel prices. If you do travel, never use shared SUVs (they push timing in risky weather) and always carry travel insurance.
Which month has the cheapest hotel rates in Darjeeling?
July and August have the cheapest rates of the year - hotels drop 40-50% to attract any traveller during peak monsoon. After that, January 10-31 is the next cheapest window with 30-40% off and far better weather. Last week of September offers near-peak weather at 25-35% off.
When is the rhododendron bloom in Darjeeling?
Rhododendrons begin blooming in early March, peak in April, and continue till mid-May. The best bloom corridor is the route from Maneybhanjyang to Sandakphu through Singalila National Park. If you want the full bloom impact, plan for the second or third week of April.
Is one week enough to experience Darjeeling fully?
Five to seven days is the ideal window - 3 days in Darjeeling town for the classic circuit, 1-2 days in an offbeat village (Lamahatta, Lepchajagat or Tinchuley), and 1 day for Mirik or a Singalila side-trip. If you want the Sandakphu trek as well, plan for 10 days total.
