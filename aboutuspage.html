Darjeeling

mist, tea, and the slow train that time forgot

Everyone's been to Mall Road. Very few have had breakfast at 5am in a bakery that's been running since 1947, or walked through a tea estate before the pickers arrived. Darjeeling is more a feeling than a town. The Raj never quite left. The fog never really lifts. And the chai — look, it spoils you for life.

Sikkim

where the mountains hold their breath when the monks chant

Sikkim will change the way you understand silence. Here, prayer flags carry more weight than most of what you'll say this month. Monasteries actually function as monasteries — not stops on a tourist circuit. And Kanchenjunga will sit there, just outside your window, ignoring everyone, as it has for all of time.

Himachal

apple orchards, pine forests, and villages where nobody's in a hurry

Please, please skip Manali's Mall Road. It's not what Himachal is. Himachal is Jibhi's wooden homes and the smell of pine smoke at 6pm. It's Tirthan, where you trade your cell signal for trout fresh from the river. It's Tabo, a 1,000-year-old monastery most travelers have never heard of. Three weeks in Himachal and you'll forget every city you've ever lived in.

✦Jibhi & Banjarwooden villages, waterfall swims, zero crowds
✦Tirthan Valleythe "no signal" zone we keep quiet about

Uttarakhand

if Himachal is your first love, Uttarakhand is the one that marries you

Uttarakhand is where the Ganges begins, which should tell you something. It's trek country — Har Ki Dun, Valley of Flowers, Roopkund — but it's also quiet mountain towns that feel held in place by faith. Chopta at dawn. Munsiyari's five peaks from your bed. Auli when it snows. Uttarakhand doesn't try to impress you. It just is.

✦Chopta & Tungnaththe world's highest Shiva temple, at 3,680m
✦Munsiyarifive peaks from your bedroom, no filter
✦Valley of Flowersmonsoon only — 300+ blooms, 500 butterflies
✦Har Ki Dunthe Stairway to Heaven, said the Pandavas
