Where to Eat in Darjeeling: A Culture-Led Food Guide

Four cultures on a single plate - Gorkha, Tibetan, Lepcha and Anglo-Indian. Here's what to order, where to sit, what to drink and the dishes you absolutely cannot leave town without trying.

Darjeeling does not have one cuisine. It has four, on a single plate. The Gorkha Nepali community brings sel roti, gundruk and aloo dum. The Tibetan refugee community brings momos, thukpa and the kind of pork dishes monks have been cooking for generations. The indigenous Lepcha people contribute nettle soups and fermented greens you'll find nowhere else in India. And the British, who arrived in the 1800s and never quite left in spirit, still run bakeries where you can order a slice of plum cake at 11 AM with a pot of first-flush Darjeeling tea. This guide is built the way we'd walk you through the food ourselves - culture first, dishes second, restaurants third, and the drinks (including the legendary Tongba) right at the end.

01 · The culture

The four cultures behind Darjeeling food

Before you pick a restaurant, you need to know where Darjeeling food comes from. The town has been a meeting point for four distinct communities for over 150 years - and every dish on every menu in town has its roots in one of these four kitchens. Once you understand the origin of what you're eating, you'll start ordering smarter.

The largest community

Gorkha & Nepali

The majority community of Darjeeling. Gorkha and Nepali kitchens brought aloo dum, sel roti, gundruk, kinema (fermented soybean), sukuti (dried meat), thakali thali, and the famous Phaapar ko Roti (buckwheat roti). Rooted in subsistence farming - lots of fermented vegetables and preserved meats.

Signature: Aloo dum + sel roti, gundruk soup
The 1959 exile

Tibetan

When Tibetans crossed the border after 1959, they brought momo, thukpa, thenthuk, sha phaley, tingmo (steamed buns), gyathuk, butter tea (po cha) and the deep love for pork that now defines Darjeeling streetside food. The Tibetan Refugee Self-Help Centre (1959) is where this lineage is most alive.

Signature: Pork momos, thukpa, sha phaley
The indigenous people

Lepcha

The original inhabitants of the Darjeeling-Sikkim region. Lepcha cuisine is hyper-local and seasonal - foraged nettle (sisnu) soup, fern fronds (niguro) curry, bamboo-shoot pork, fermented millet drinks. Rarely found on tourist menus, but homestays in Lamahatta and Tinchuley still serve it.

Signature: Sisnu soup, niguro fern curry, bamboo shoot pork
The colonial heritage

Anglo-Indian & British

The British arrived to set up tea estates and stayed for over a century. The bakery culture they left behind is unbroken - fruit cakes, plum cakes, sausage rolls, fish and chips, lemon tarts, roast pork, and the afternoon-tea ritual still served at Windamere with silver tongs and white-gloved waiters.

Signature: Plum cake, roast pork, afternoon tea

Bonus communities: Bengali tourist heritage (fish curries on every menu), Bhutia (Sikkimese), and recent Naga & South Indian additions for the expat workforce. But the four above shaped the original food identity.

02 · The dishes

16 must-try dishes in Darjeeling

Sorted by community origin. Each dish has a colour-coded tag showing where it came from. Pick at least one from each lineage during your trip.

Tibetan

Pork Momos

/moh-moh/

Steamed or fried dumplings filled with juicy spiced minced pork. The classic order comes with a fiery red chilli sauce (achar). The single most ordered dish in all of Darjeeling.

Where: Kunga Restaurant, Hot Stimulating Cafe, Dekevas
Tibetan

Thukpa

/took-pa/

A hot Tibetan noodle soup with vegetables, egg or meat, swimming in a clear broth flavoured with garlic, ginger and Sichuan pepper. The original cold-weather comfort food of these mountains.

Where: Kunga, Noryang Tibetan Kitchen, Singalila Restaurant
Tibetan

Thenthuk

/then-took/

Hand-pulled, irregular flat noodles in a brothy soup with vegetables and meat. Earthier and more textural than thukpa. A Tibetan grandmother's recipe most cafes don't bother to make well.

Where: Hot Stimulating Cafe, Noryang Tibetan Kitchen
Tibetan

Sha Phaley

/shah-pa-lay/

Tibetan stuffed bread - dough wrapped around spiced minced meat and cabbage, deep-fried until golden. Crispy outside, juicy inside. Best eaten hot with sweet chilli or vinegar dip.

Where: Kunga, Noryang, Tibetan Refugee Centre cafeteria
Tibetan

Tingmo

/ting-mo/

Soft, fluffy steamed bread (think Tibetan dim sum bun) served alongside curries or stews. Tastes like edible cloud. Made fresh on the spot in good Tibetan kitchens.

Where: Kunga, Hot Stimulating Cafe
Tibetan

Po Cha (Butter Tea)

/poh-cha/

Tibetan butter tea - made with yak butter, salt and tea leaves churned into a thick, savoury drink. Acquired taste. Less a beverage and more an edible warmth at sub-zero altitudes.

Where: Monasteries on butter-tea-offering days, Tibetan homestays
Gorkha

Aloo Dum with Sel Roti

/aa-loo-dum/

Spicy-tangy potato curry served with sel roti (a slightly sweet, deep-fried ring-shaped rice bread). The Nepali Sunday breakfast you'll never forget. The chilli is real - ask for "kam mirchi" if you want it milder.

Where: Hasty Tasty, Singalila Restaurant, local breakfast stalls
Gorkha

Gundruk Soup

/gun-drook/

Fermented leafy greens (usually radish or mustard tops) boiled into a sour, tangy, slightly pungent soup. Sounds intimidating, tastes amazing. The Nepali equivalent of kimchi soup.

Where: Singalila Restaurant, Hasty Tasty, Lamahatta homestays
Gorkha

Sukuti

/soo-koo-tee/

Dried, spiced buffalo or pork meat - the Nepali jerky. Served as a snack with raksi (rice liquor) or as part of a Gorkha thali. Chewy, intense, deeply savoury. A bar-snack masterpiece.

Where: Singalila Restaurant, local roadside bars on Gandhi Road
Gorkha

Thakali Thali

/tha-ka-lee/

A full Nepali platter - rice, dal, vegetable curry, gundruk, sukuti, achar (pickle), and curd. The complete soul-food meal of the Gorkha kitchen. Best ordered as lunch.

Where: Singalila Restaurant, Kathmandu Kitchen, Hasty Tasty
Lepcha

Sisnu (Nettle) Soup

/sis-noo/

Boiled nettle leaves cooked into a deeply green, mineral-rich soup. Tastes earthy, slightly nutty, almost like spinach with personality. A traditional Lepcha and Limbu dish.

Where: Lamahatta and Tinchuley homestays, Lepcha-run cafes
Lepcha

Niguro (Fern) Curry

/nee-gu-ro/

Wild fern fronds, foraged in spring, lightly stir-fried with garlic, ginger and dried chillies. Crunchy, slightly bitter, deeply seasonal. Available only March-June.

Where: Singalila Restaurant, offbeat village homestays
Lepcha

Bamboo Shoot Pork

/tama/

Slow-cooked pork with fermented bamboo shoots, slightly sour, deeply meaty. A Lepcha-Limbu cold-weather classic. Order with steamed rice and a pickle.

Where: Singalila Restaurant, traditional village kitchens
Anglo-Indian

Roast Pork at Glenary's

/glen-uh-reez/

Slow-roasted pork with crackling skin, brown gravy, mashed potatoes and seasonal vegetables. The British colonial Sunday roast, still served the same way since 1885. Order their pork chops too.

Where: Glenary's Restaurant & Pub (Nehru Road)
Anglo-Indian

Plum Cake & Fruit Cake

Christmas cake

Dense, fragrant, brandy-soaked fruit cake made the proper Anglo-Indian way. Glenary's has been baking it since 1885. The single best edible souvenir you can take home from Darjeeling.

Where: Glenary's bakery, Keventer's, Frank Ross Cafe
Anglo-Indian

First-Flush Darjeeling Tea

/first-flush/

The most prized black tea in the world - harvested March-April, light golden in colour, with floral aromatics and a distinct muscatel finish. Drink it steeped 3-4 minutes, no milk, no sugar. The tea you've been missing your whole life.

Where: Nathmulls Tea Room, Happy Valley Tea Estate shop, Goodricke
Insider order
If you only have a single meal in Darjeeling, walk into Kunga and order: half-plate pork momos (steam), one thukpa, one sha phaley, one tingmo. Comes to under ₹500 per person and covers the full Tibetan flavour spectrum.

03 · The institutions

Heritage restaurants - the institutions

Some places in Darjeeling have been feeding travellers for over a century. These four are non-negotiable for first-time visitors.

Glenary's
Since 1885
Location
Nehru Road

₹400 - 700
Open
6 AM - 9 PM
Rating
4.3 (21K+)

An institution since 1885. Glenary's is three things at once - a bakery on the ground floor (where locals queue every morning for buns and cakes), a multi-cuisine restaurant on the first floor (with Kanchenjunga views on clear days), and Joey's Pub on the upper level (Darjeeling's most beloved bar). Get the cinnamon rolls, sausage rolls, roast pork or the famous lemon tart. The upper-floor restaurant overlooks the valley.

Insider tip
Visit Glenary's bakery before 9 AM for the best selection - cinnamon rolls and cream-filled buns sell out fast. The plum cake (₹480 per 500g) is the single best edible souvenir from Darjeeling.

08 · The local drink

Local alcohol & what to actually try

Tongba. Raksi. Chhang. These are the three names every Darjeeling visitor should know. They predate the British, they're brewed in homes, and they tell you more about Gorkha culture than any food can.

🍺

Tongba

The icon · ₹60 - 150

Fermented finger millet packed into a hollow bamboo container. Hot water poured over it, then sipped through a thin bamboo straw. Mildly alcoholic, warm, slightly sour, deeply nostalgic. A 2-hour drinking ritual, not a quick shot. Refill the hot water 3-4 times.

🍷

Raksi

The strong one · ₹80 - 200

Distilled liquor from rice or millet. Clear, sharp, around 30-40% ABV. Drunk neat in small glasses with sukuti (dried meat) or sandhe-ko-achaar pickles. The Gorkha equivalent of country-distilled whisky - smoother than you'd expect.

🍻

Chhang

The everyday one · ₹50 - 120

Fermented millet or rice beverage - milky, slightly sweet, lightly alcoholic. The everyday drink of Lepcha and Bhutia households. Served chilled in summer, warm in winter. The most beginner-friendly Himalayan booze.

Where to try authentic Tongba

Tongba isn't sold in heritage hotels or tourist restaurants. To drink the real thing, head to small Gorkha-owned bars on Gandhi Road, Robertson Road, near the Old Super Market, and especially in Lebong. The drink is served in a traditional tongba (the bamboo container itself gives the drink its name) with a thin metal-tipped bamboo straw called a pipsing.

If you're not comfortable walking into a local bar, ask your homestay host (especially in Lamahatta or Tinchuley) to arrange a Tongba evening at their home. It's a deeply cultural experience.

Bars worth visiting

  • Joey's Pub (above Glenary's) - the most popular bar in Darjeeling, generous pours, live music on weekends, mid-budget pricing
  • Buzz Cafe Bar - cocktails, draft beer, good for groups
  • The Park Restaurant Bar - quieter, more conservative crowd
  • Glass Bar at Taj Chia Kutir (Kurseong) - tea-infused cocktails and craft mixers if you're staying at the Taj
Drinking culture note
Darjeeling's drinking culture is unhurried and social. Tongba in particular is a 2-hour group ritual - a single tongba is shared with 1-2 refills of hot water, accompanied by slow conversation and dry snacks. Not a beverage to be rushed.

09 · The tea

Darjeeling tea - the proper experience

You're in the world capital of premium tea. Drinking masala chai with milk here is a missed opportunity. Here's how to actually do it right.

The four flushes

  • First flush (March-April) - light, floral, golden-tinted. The most prized, the most expensive, the most exported. The "champagne of teas". ₹1,500-5,000 per 100g for top-grade.
  • Second flush (May-June) - the famous "muscatel" character. Richer, slightly fruitier, deeper amber. Equally beloved by tea connoisseurs.
  • Monsoon flush (July-September) - everyday drinking tea, briskest of the four. Mostly sold as bulk.
  • Autumn flush (October-November) - mellow, slightly nutty, deeper colour. The cosy-evening tea.

Where to taste tea properly

Nathmulls Tea Room on Laden La Road is the most-recommended tea-tasting stop. They serve flights of 4-5 estate teas with proper white-porcelain cups, pour-over technique, and a knowledgeable host who explains origin and tasting notes. Around ₹200-500 per flight.

Happy Valley Tea Estate shop sells direct-from-factory teas. You can taste before you buy. First-flush varieties become available in mid-April.

Goodricke Tea Boutique on Mall Road - a more commercial but reliable buy-and-go option.

How to drink it
Steep 1 teaspoon of first-flush in 200ml hot water (NOT boiling - 80°C is ideal) for exactly 3 minutes. No milk. No sugar. Drink it slowly with a slice of plum cake. This is the only way you'll understand why Darjeeling tea has held its global reputation for 150 years.

12 · The questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the famous food of Darjeeling?
Darjeeling's most famous foods are pork momos, thukpa (Tibetan noodle soup), thenthuk, Nepali aloo dum with sel roti, churpi (yak cheese), gundruk soup, sha phaley and Darjeeling first-flush tea. The cuisine is a blend of Gorkha, Tibetan, Lepcha and Anglo-Indian influences shaped by the town's 150-year history as a cultural meeting point.
Which is the best restaurant in Darjeeling?
The most loved restaurants are Glenary's (heritage bakery and multi-cuisine since 1885), Keventer's (iconic rooftop terrace), Kunga Restaurant (best Tibetan, particularly pork momos), Sonam's Kitchen (homestyle breakfasts), Hot Stimulating Cafe (Tibetan family-run thenthuk), Penang Restaurant (Southeast Asian) and Singalila Restaurant (authentic Nepali home cooking).
What is Tongba and where to try it in Darjeeling?
Tongba is a traditional Gorkha-Limbu millet beer served in a bamboo container with hot water poured over fermented finger millet and sipped through a thin bamboo straw. It's the most iconic alcoholic drink of the Darjeeling-Sikkim region. Try authentic Tongba at small Gorkha-owned bars on Gandhi Road, Robertson Road and in Lebong - typically ₹60-150 per serving with 3-4 refills of hot water included.
Which cafe in Darjeeling is most Instagrammable?
The most photogenic cafes are Revolver (vintage Royal Enfield decor), Mehrab's Cafe (rooftop Kanchenjunga view), Tea Bar by Nathmulls (aesthetic estate-tea bar), Glenary's upper floor (heritage wood interiors) and The Buzz Cafe (cosy fairy-lit corners). All are good for a slow coffee + photo session.
Is there McDonald's or KFC in Darjeeling?
No major international fast-food chains operate inside Darjeeling town. The local taxi union and town authority have historically resisted big-brand chains. You'll find Cafe Coffee Day on Mall Road and small chains in Siliguri (Bagdogra airport route), but the Darjeeling food scene remains independent restaurant-led.
Are there good vegetarian restaurants in Darjeeling?
Yes. Grains Pure Veg by Udaan Nirvana is the highest-rated pure-vegetarian restaurant in town (5.0 on TripAdvisor). Sonam's Kitchen offers excellent vegetarian breakfast options. Most Tibetan restaurants offer vegetable momos, vegetable thukpa and vegetable thenthuk. Mainstream restaurants like Glenary's and Keventer's have full veg menus. Darjeeling is very vegetarian-friendly.
What is the cheapest place to eat in Darjeeling?
For budget eats under ₹200 per meal, head to the Chowk Bazaar momo stalls (₹60-100 per plate), Hasty Tasty (₹100-250 thali), Dekevas (₹200-400), Kathmandu Kitchen (₹150-300) and local food joints on Robertson Road. Government tourist lodges also serve cheap thali meals at ₹150-200.
What is special to drink in Darjeeling besides tea?
Tongba (millet beer in bamboo container), Raksi (distilled rice/millet liquor) and Chhang (fermented millet beverage) are the three traditional Himalayan drinks. Also try Tibetan butter tea (po cha) at monasteries, and tea-infused cocktails at Joey's Pub or the Glass Bar at Taj Chia Kutir.
Where to buy Darjeeling tea to take home?
Nathmulls Tea Room (Laden La Road) has the widest estate-grade selection. Happy Valley Tea Estate shop sells direct from the factory at the cheapest prices. Goodricke Tea Boutique on Mall Road is a reliable buy-and-go option. Avoid the generic souvenir-shop teas - they're often blended bulk grade, not single-estate.
Are there any rooftop restaurants in Darjeeling?
Yes. The famous rooftop restaurants are Keventer's terrace (the iconic photograph spot), Mehrab's Cafe (rooftop Kanchenjunga view), Glenary's upper floor (glass-windowed valley view) and the Cafe at Cedar Inn (upper Jalapahar ridge). All offer mountain views on clear days.
Is Lepcha food easy to find in Darjeeling?
Authentic Lepcha cuisine (sisnu nettle soup, niguro fern curry, bamboo shoot pork) is mostly served in offbeat village homestays in Lamahatta, Tinchuley, Lepchajagat and Chatakpur. In town, Singalila Restaurant is your best bet for Lepcha-influenced dishes. Most mainstream restaurants don't carry it on the regular menu.
