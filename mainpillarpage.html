01 · The introduction

Why Darjeeling?

Darjeeling isn't just another hill station. It's a small town that sits somewhere between a colonial British holiday and a quiet Himalayan dream.

Perched at the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas at 6,710 feet, this little town wears many crowns at once. It is the world capital of premium tea - home to the celebrated first-flush and second-flush varieties exported globally, protected under a Geographical Indication tag and often called "the champagne of teas".

It's also the gateway to one of only two UNESCO World Heritage mountain railways in India - the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, lovingly known as the toy train.

And on a clear morning, it offers what travellers across the world call the best mountain sunrise view in India - the moment Kanchenjunga turns gold above a sea of clouds at Tiger Hill.

❦

But beyond the postcards, Darjeeling is a culturally layered town.

You'll see Tibetan monasteries flying prayer flags right next to Anglo-Indian bakeries that have been baking the same fruit cakes since the 1800s. You'll hear Nepali, Tibetan, Bengali, Hindi and English spoken on the same Mall Road in a single afternoon. You'll find Buddhist gompas, Hindu temples and English-Gothic churches within walking distance of each other.

The food carries echoes of all of these cultures - hot pork momos served in tin-roofed cafes, churpi cheese pulled from local kitchens, English afternoon tea poured at heritage hotels established before independence.

What makes Darjeeling truly special for us at Offbeat Peak is what lies just beyond the well-known sightseeing circuit. Tucked behind Tiger Hill and Mall Road are forest villages like Lamahatta, cloud-wrapped hamlets like Lepchajagat, and tea-tourism homestays in Tinchuley where you can pluck your own tea leaves before breakfast.

The town also serves as the starting point for one of India's most scenic high-altitude treks - Sandakphu in Singalila National Park, from where, on a perfect day, you can see four of the world's five highest peaks lined up across the horizon.

02 · The when

Best time to visit Darjeeling

The best time to visit Darjeeling is from October to December and again from March to May.

These two windows give you the clearest views of Kanchenjunga, pleasant daytime temperatures, and the kind of fresh mountain air that makes the trip worth every photo. That said, every season here has a personality of its own.

October to December - peak Kanchenjunga season

This is the best time to visit if mountain views are your priority. Post-monsoon clouds clear out by early October and the sky stays sharp and blue through November.

Daytime temperatures range between 10 and 18°C, while nights can drop to 4-7°C. Tiger Hill sunrise has its highest success rate during this window. On lucky mornings you might even catch a glimpse of Everest in the far distance.

By December, light snow becomes possible at higher zones like Sandakphu. Pack layered woollens, thermals and a windproof jacket.

March to May - spring and rhododendron bloom

Spring transforms Darjeeling into a riot of pink, red and white rhododendron blooms, especially around Singalila National Park and the road to Sandakphu.

Temperatures are comfortable at 12-20°C. This is also the most popular tourist season, so book hotels at least 4-6 weeks in advance. April-May overlaps with school holidays - tour packages get sold out fast.

June to September - we don't recommend this

Heavy, unpredictable rainfall. Landslides on NH-55 are common, the toy train often gets cancelled, and Kanchenjunga hides behind clouds for days.

If you absolutely must travel during this window, late September has better weather and significantly lower hotel prices. The hills are at their most vivid green and waterfalls are at peak flow.

January to February - the offbeat traveller's secret

Tourist crowds thin out dramatically, hotel rates drop by 30-40 percent, and on lucky days you might see Darjeeling under a blanket of snow.

Day temperatures hover around 5-10°C, but nights can fall close to zero. Pack heavy woollens, gloves and a thermal layer.

Our quick pick
For first-time visitors who want the postcard Darjeeling experience with Kanchenjunga clearly visible, plan for the last week of October through third week of November, or the first three weeks of April. We've personally tested both. They're the sweet spots.

03 · The journey

How to reach Darjeeling

Darjeeling doesn't have its own airport or major railway station, which catches a lot of first-time travellers off guard.

The town is connected through three transit hubs in the plains - Bagdogra Airport, New Jalpaiguri Railway Station (NJP), and Siliguri Bus Terminal. From any of these three, you'll have a 70-80 km uphill drive of around 3-4 hours to reach Darjeeling town.

✈

By air

Bagdogra Airport (IXB), 3.5 hr cab. Direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata and Guwahati.

⏲

By train

New Jalpaiguri (NJP), one of the most connected stations in eastern India. Daily trains from Kolkata, Delhi, Patna and Guwahati.

🚘

By road

How to reach Darjeeling by air

The nearest airport is Bagdogra Airport (IXB), near Siliguri. It has direct daily flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad and Guwahati - operated by IndiGo, Air India, SpiceJet and Akasa Air.

Flight times range from 1 hour (Kolkata) to 2.5 hours (Mumbai). Once you land, you have three onward options:

  • Pre-paid taxi - private cab ₹3,500-4,500
  • Shared SUV - ₹350-450 per seat, runs when full
  • Private cab booked through your hotel in advance

The 70 km road climbs through tea gardens, the town of Kurseong, and Ghoom before reaching Darjeeling. Total travel time is around 3.5 hours.

₹
Smart saver Skip the airport counter cabs. Walk 100 metres outside the airport, where shared SUVs charge ₹350-450 per seat instead of ₹3,500+. If you're solo or in a couple, you save ₹2,500-3,000 right there.

How to reach Darjeeling by train

The nearest railway station is New Jalpaiguri (NJP), one of the most well-connected stations in eastern India.

Daily express trains run from Kolkata (Darjeeling Mail and Padatik Express both arrive at NJP early morning), Delhi (Rajdhani Express, NDLS-NJP Special, Bihar Sampark Kranti), Guwahati, Patna and most major Indian cities.

From NJP you can take a shared jeep from the station forecourt (₹300-400 per seat), a private cab (₹3,500-4,500), or board the legendary Darjeeling Himalayan Railway - the toy train all the way up (7-8 hours, not recommended for time-pressed travellers).

₹
Smart saver The overnight Darjeeling Mail from Kolkata reaches NJP at 8:00 AM. Catch a 9:00 AM shared SUV - you'll be in Darjeeling by 1:00 PM with the whole afternoon free. Tickets ₹500 (3AC) to ₹2,400 (1AC).

How to reach Darjeeling by road

If you're already in the Northeast, in Sikkim, or in nearby hill destinations like Kalimpong and Pelling, road is the most convenient option.

From Siliguri's Tenzing Norgay Bus Terminal, shared SUVs and Tata Sumos run all day (₹180-300 per seat). From Gangtok the road via Teesta River is around 100 km and takes 4 hours. From Kalimpong, Darjeeling is just 50 km and 2.5 hours away.

Self-drive is technically possible but we don't recommend it for first-timers - the road is narrow, full of hairpin bends, and shared with the toy train tracks in several stretches.

Hassle-free travel hack
Book a morning flight to Bagdogra so you reach Darjeeling by early evening with daylight to spare. Avoid arriving after dark - the mountain roads have minimal street lighting, and most homestays are tucked into narrow lanes that are hard to navigate at night.

04 · The places

Top 12 places to visit in Darjeeling

Darjeeling has more sightseeing spots than you can possibly cover in a single trip. Below is our hand-picked list of the top 12 places to visit in Darjeeling, ranked by experience value, not just popularity.

Each place gets a money-saving tip we wish someone had told us on our first trip.

01

Tiger Hill - sunrise over Kanchenjunga

⏱ 4:30 AM - 6:30 AM₹ 40-100 entry11 km from town

At 2,590 m, Tiger Hill is the highest point in the Darjeeling area and the most iconic sunrise spot in eastern India. On a clear morning you can see Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), and on the rarest days, even Mount Everest.

₹
Smart saver Skip the ₹100 paid viewing tower - the free roadside spots 200 metres downhill have the exact same view. Use shared jeeps (₹200 round trip per person) instead of a private taxi (₹1,800). Avoid Sundays in peak season - it gets packed.
02

Batasia Loop & War Memorial

⏱ 7:00 AM - 6:00 PM₹ 20 entry5 km from town

A spiral engineering marvel where the toy train descends a 1,000 ft drop through a beautifully landscaped garden. The Gorkha War Memorial sits at the centre - a peaceful spot for a 30-minute stop.

₹
Smart saver Every Tiger Hill shared jeep stops at Batasia Loop for free on the way back - you don't need a separate trip. Combine both in one morning circuit.
03

Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (Toy Train)

⏱ 2 hr joyride₹ 1,300-1,600UNESCO World Heritage

The Darjeeling-Ghoom-Darjeeling joyride is a 2-hour round trip on a steam or diesel engine that passes through Batasia Loop and Ghoom (India's highest railway station). The steam-engine version is more atmospheric but harder to book.

₹
Smart saver Book directly on IRCTC (₹1,300-1,600), not through hotel agents who add ₹500-800 markup. Book 7-10 days ahead in peak season. The 8:00 AM and 10:40 AM slots have the best clear-weather odds.
04

Mall Road & Chowrasta

⏱ 24x7Free entryTown centre

The social heart of Darjeeling - a pedestrian-only square ringed with colonial-era buildings, cafes, bookstores and souvenir shops. Glenary's bakery and Keventer's terrace are within walking distance. Evenings are alive with street musicians.

₹
Smart saver Pony rides (₹500 for 10 mins) are heavily overpriced. Skip them - the real magic is just walking Chowrasta at sunset with a Keventer's hot chocolate. Souvenir shops on Mall Road mark up 40-60% over what the Tibetan Refugee Centre charges.
05

Happy Valley Tea Estate

⏱ 8:00 AM - 4:30 PM₹ 100-3003 km from Mall Road

Founded in 1854, Happy Valley is the only working tea estate inside Darjeeling town open for guided tours. Watch the full process - plucking, withering, rolling, fermenting - and taste authentic first-flush, second-flush and autumn-flush teas.

₹
Smart saver The ₹100 self-walk gives you the same garden views as the ₹300 guided tour. Take the guided tour only if you genuinely want the processing demo. Buy tea here, not Mall Road - it's 25% cheaper at source.
06

Padmaja Naidu Zoological Park & HMI

⏱ 8:30 AM - 4:30 PM₹ 120 comboClosed Thursdays

Home to snow leopards, red pandas, Himalayan black bears and Tibetan wolves. Plan at least 2-3 hours. The Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI) sits inside the same compound with a brilliant Everest summit museum.

₹
Smart saver Ask for the combo ticket at the counter - zoo + HMI museum together costs ₹120 instead of ₹180 separately. Visit before noon - red pandas are most active in cooler hours.
07

Japanese Peace Pagoda

⏱ 4:30 AM - 7:00 PMFree entry2 km from Mall Road

Built by the Nipponzan-Myohoji order in 1992. A gleaming white stupa offering one of the calmest viewpoints in Darjeeling. Attend the morning or evening prayer drumming - one of the most meditative experiences in the region.

₹
Smart saver Walk down from Mall Road instead of taking a cab (₹200 saved). The 40-minute downhill walk passes through quiet residential lanes. Take a cab back up if your legs aren't game.
08

Ghoom Monastery (Yiga Choeling)

⏱ 6:00 AM - 6:00 PMFree entry8 km from town

Built in 1850, one of the oldest Tibetan Buddhist monasteries in the Darjeeling region. The 15 ft statue of Maitreya Buddha inside is its main draw. Donations welcome but not required.

₹
Smart saver Get off the toy train at Ghoom Station - the monastery is a 5-minute walk. You combine your ₹1,300 toy train ride with a free monastery visit, saving the separate cab fare.
09

Rock Garden & Ganga Maya Park

⏱ 8:30 AM - 5:00 PM₹ 2010 km from town

Two adjoining parks with tiered waterfalls, hand-built rock seats and a small boating lake. Perfect for a relaxed half-day, especially with family.

₹
Smart saver Private cab to Rock Garden costs ₹1,500+ round trip. Take a shared SUV from Chowk Bazaar (₹250-300 per seat). Or pair it with a Mirik day trip if you're already heading that way.
10

Singalila National Park

⏱ Day or trek₹ 200 permitIndo-Nepal border

78 sq km along the Indo-Nepal border. Home to red pandas, leopards and 120+ bird species. The starting point of the legendary Sandakphu trek.

₹
Smart saver Sharing a Land Rover with 5 strangers (₹1,200-1,500 per person to Sandakphu) saves 60% over a private vehicle. Join group trek itineraries on platforms like Indiahikes for fixed-departure prices.
11

Observatory Hill & Mahakal Temple

⏱ 5:00 AM - 7:00 PMFree entry10 min walk from Chowrasta

Sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists. Mornings are peaceful with monks and locals offering prayers side by side. The viewing platform gives you one of the best free Kanchenjunga views in town.

₹
Smart saver On cloudy Tiger Hill mornings, Observatory Hill is your free backup plan for sunrise. Walk up by 5:00 AM, free Kanchenjunga view, zero cab fare.
12

Tibetan Refugee Self-Help Centre

⏱ 9:00 AM - 4:30 PMFree entryClosed Sundays

Set up in 1959 after the Tibetan exile. Watch live carpet weaving, wood carving and Thangka painting. Buy authentic handicrafts directly from the artisans.

₹
Smart saver Yak-wool shawls, prayer flags and Tibetan jewellery here are 30-40% cheaper than the same items on Mall Road. Don't buy souvenirs anywhere else in Darjeeling - this is the spot.

05 · The hidden side

Offbeat & hidden gems near Darjeeling

Our brand is called Offbeat Peak for a reason. Beyond the standard 7-point tour that every Darjeeling tour package sells, there are quiet villages, untouched viewpoints, and tea-tourism homestays that most travellers never hear about.

Below are six offbeat places near Darjeeling we strongly recommend if you have an extra day or two. We've stayed in each.

Lamahatta - the pine-forest village

A tiny eco-tourism village 23 km from Darjeeling at 5,700 ft, Lamahatta is wrapped in pine and dhupi forests.

There's a forested walking trail leading to a small sacred pond at the top, and a handful of beautifully kept homestays where you can stay overnight for ₹1,500-3,000.

₹
Pro tip Book directly through the village WhatsApp groups (we have the contacts) - you'll save 20-25% over OTA prices and get home-cooked Nepali meals included.

Lepchajagat - cloud-wrapped forest hamlet

Just 19 km from Darjeeling at 6,900 ft, Lepchajagat is a forest hamlet often hidden in clouds for half the day.

The West Bengal Forest Development Corporation runs wooden cottages here deep inside an oak and rhododendron forest. No Wi-Fi, almost no mobile signal, no shops - just the sound of wind through trees.

₹
Pro tip Book WBFDC cottages directly through their government website (around ₹2,000/night) instead of agents who quote ₹3,500-4,500. Reservations open 90 days in advance.

Tinchuley - the tea-tourism village

Sitting at 5,800 ft, 32 km from Darjeeling, Tinchuley is a tea-tourism village built around organic farming homestays.

You can pluck your own tea leaves, learn how to process them, and join the village families for traditional Nepali meals served on copper plates.

₹
Pro tip Tinchuley homestays charge ₹1,800-2,500/night with ALL meals included. Cheaper than a 3-star hotel in town and far more memorable.

Mirik - the lake town

Famous for the boat-shaped Sumendu Lake, Mirik is a relaxed lake-town 50 km from Darjeeling. Pony rides, paddle boating, an orange orchard, and the Bokar Monastery on a nearby hill make this a popular day trip.

₹
Pro tip Reach by 9:00 AM before tour buses arrive - the lake is yours. Extend 5 km further to Pashupati Nagar at the Nepal border for cheaper electronics and snacks.

Sandakphu - the trekker's paradise

At 11,930 ft, Sandakphu is the highest peak in West Bengal. On a clear day you'll see four of the world's five highest peaks - Everest, Kanchenjunga, Lhotse and Makalu - lined up across the horizon.

The trek takes 5 to 7 days, ideal in October-November and March-April. Land Rovers also run for those who can't trek.

Chatakpur - the eco-village

A model eco-village at 7,887 ft, 27 km from Darjeeling, Chatakpur has just 20-odd households and is run as a community tourism project. Stay in eco-cottages run by the village forest committee for ₹1,500-2,500/night.

06 · The experiences

Best things to do in Darjeeling

Beyond ticking off sightseeing places, here are the experiences that turn a Darjeeling trip into something you'll genuinely remember.

Ride the UNESCO World Heritage toy train

The Darjeeling-Ghoom joyride is non-negotiable for first-time visitors. Tickets ₹1,300-1,600. Book through IRCTC, never via hotel agents.

Watch sunrise from Tiger Hill

Wake up at 3:30 AM. Mount Kanchenjunga turning gold over a sea of clouds is the photo that has sold Darjeeling for over a century.

Take a guided tea estate tour

A walk through Happy Valley or the more upscale Glenburn Tea Estate teaches you how Darjeeling tea is plucked, processed and graded. Glenburn full-day plantation experiences with lunch run ₹3,500-4,500 per person.

Trek to Sandakphu

The 5-7 day Sandakphu-Phalut trek crosses Singalila National Park with overnight stays in trekkers' huts at Tumling, Kalipokhri and Sandakphu.

Cable car ride at Darjeeling Ropeway

India's oldest passenger cable car, a 45-minute ride over tea gardens and valleys. Tickets ₹350. Go in the morning for clearer views.

Try traditional Tibetan and Nepali food

Pork momos at Kunga, thenthuk at Hot Stimulating Cafe, a slice of cake at Glenary's with a cup of Darjeeling tea.

Shop at Tibetan Refugee Centre

First-flush tea, handwoven shawls, prayer flags and yak-wool products - 30-40% cheaper than Mall Road.

08 · The stay

Where to stay in Darjeeling

Darjeeling has accommodation for every budget - from ₹600 hostels to ₹25,000 heritage suites.

The most popular areas are Mall Road, Chowrasta, Jalapahar, Gandhi Road and Robertson Road. We always recommend staying within 1 km of Mall Road on your first visit, since most sightseeing places and cafes are within walking distance.

Budget stays (₹600-1,500/night)

Backpacker hostels like Hostelavie Darjeeling, Tripping Walrus and Zostel offer clean dorms and basic private rooms. Most are near Gandhi Road. Wi-Fi is patchy but breakfast is usually included.

Mid-range hotels (₹2,000-5,000/night)

The best value-to-experience category. We recommend Summit Grace, Hotel Sonar Bangla, Hotel Seven Seventeen, Sterling Darjeeling and Hotel North Star. Request north-facing rooms for Kanchenjunga views.

Heritage & luxury (₹8,000-25,000+/night)

Darjeeling has some of the most historic colonial heritage hotels in India. Windamere Hotel on Observatory Hill (1841), Glenburn Tea Estate, Mayfair Darjeeling (former summer palace of the Maharaja of Nazargunj), Cedar Inn and Elgin Darjeeling.

Offbeat homestays (₹1,500-4,000/night)

For something quieter, book a homestay in Lamahatta, Lepchajagat, Tinchuley, Takdah or Chatakpur. WhatsApp booking is common in this region and often gets you a 10-15% discount over OTA prices.

₹
Hassle-free hack For first-time visitors: book a mid-range mountain-facing hotel near Mall Road for the first two nights, then move to an offbeat homestay in Lamahatta or Tinchuley for the third night. The contrast between buzzing town and silent forest is what makes the trip truly memorable.

09 · The food

Where to eat in Darjeeling

Darjeeling's food scene is a delicious mix of Tibetan, Nepali, Anglo-Indian and Bengali influences. Here are the spots you cannot miss - in our personal order of priority.

Glenary's

A Darjeeling institution since 1885. Order the cinnamon rolls, sausage rolls, fish and chips, or the famous lemon tart. The upper-floor restaurant overlooks Kanchenjunga on clear mornings. ₹400-700/head.

Keventer's

The iconic terrace restaurant on Nehru Road from Satyajit Ray's "Aranyer Din Ratri" days. Order the breakfast platter with ham, sausages, eggs and toast. ₹350-500/head.

Kunga Restaurant

For authentic Tibetan food. Their pork momos, thukpa and gyathuk are widely considered the best in town. ₹150-300/head.

Hot Stimulating Cafe

A tiny cafe run by a Tibetan family near Mall Road. The thenthuk and Tibetan butter tea are excellent. Cosy seating, slow service - go when you have time.

Sonam's Kitchen

A hidden gem near Dr. Zakir Hussain Road. Homestyle Tibetan and continental food. Breakfasts (eggs benedict, pancakes) are particularly excellent.

Must-try Darjeeling dishes

  • Pork or chicken momos with the local fiery chilli sauce
  • Thukpa - hearty Tibetan noodle soup
  • Thenthuk - hand-pulled noodle soup
  • Aloo dum with sel roti - Nepali breakfast
  • Churpi - chewy yak cheese
  • Darjeeling first-flush tea - steeped, no milk, no sugar
  • Glenary's pastries - rum balls, fruit cake, dark chocolate brownie
₹
Smart saver Avoid the touristy "package meal" cafes on Mall Road. The roadside momo stalls near Chowk Bazaar serve the same Tibetan food at ₹60-100 per plate vs ₹250+ in tourist restaurants.

For a comfortable 3-day trip per person with mid-range stays and one offbeat night, plan for ₹13,000-15,000 excluding flights. Travelling as a couple usually brings per-person cost down by 25-30%.

11 · The fine print

Culture, etiquette & travel tips

Language and people

Most locals are fluent in Nepali, Hindi, English and Bengali. A simple "Namaste" or "Tashi Delek" (Tibetan greeting) will be warmly returned.

Monastery and temple etiquette

Remove your shoes before entering any prayer hall. Walk clockwise around stupas and prayer wheels. Always ask before clicking inside the inner sanctum.

Altitude and acclimatisation

Most travellers don't face AMS at 6,710 ft, but if you're going to Sandakphu (11,930 ft) or Tiger Hill early morning, stay hydrated and avoid alcohol the night before.

Mobile connectivity

Airtel and Jio work well in Darjeeling town. BSNL is the only reliable network in offbeat areas. Download offline Google Maps.

Money & UPI

Mall Road has multiple ATMs. UPI works in most cafes and hotels in town. Carry ₹3,000-5,000 in cash for Lamahatta, Tinchuley or Sandakphu.

Avoid common scams

Unauthorised taxi operators near NJP and Bagdogra quote inflated prices. Always book through the pre-paid taxi counter or your hotel.

Packing essentials

Warm jacket, thermals, sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm, comfortable trekking shoes, power bank, light backpack, reusable water bottle.

12 · The questions

Frequently asked questions

How many days are enough for Darjeeling?
For first-time visitors, three days are ideal to cover Tiger Hill, Batasia Loop, Mall Road, the toy train, Happy Valley Tea Estate and Ghoom Monastery without rushing. If you want to add offbeat places like Lamahatta, Lepchajagat or Mirik, plan for five days. For a Sandakphu trek, set aside at least seven to ten days including travel time.
What are the 7 points in a Darjeeling tour?
The classic 7-point Darjeeling tour covers Tiger Hill sunrise, Batasia Loop, Ghoom Monastery, Japanese Peace Pagoda, Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI), Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park, and a tea garden (usually Happy Valley). The whole tour takes around 5 to 7 hours by shared jeep or private cab and costs ₹200-300 per person for a shared jeep or ₹2,500-3,500 for a private vehicle.
Is 2 nights enough for Darjeeling?
Two nights are barely enough for the highlights. You can cover Tiger Hill, the 7-point tour and a Mall Road evening, but you'll have to skip the toy train, Happy Valley tea tour and any offbeat villages. We recommend at least three nights for a comfortable Darjeeling experience.
Which is the most famous place in Darjeeling?
Tiger Hill is the most famous tourist place in Darjeeling, known worldwide for sunrise views of Mount Kanchenjunga. Other equally popular spots include Mall Road (Chowrasta), Batasia Loop, the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (toy train), Padmaja Naidu Himalayan Zoological Park, Happy Valley Tea Estate and the Japanese Peace Pagoda.
Which month is the best for Darjeeling?
The best months are October, November, March, April and May. October-November gives the clearest Kanchenjunga views and post-monsoon greenery. March-May brings warmer days, rhododendron blooms and spring colours. Avoid June to early September due to monsoon and landslide risks. For snow, January and February are your best bet.
How much does a Darjeeling trip cost from Kolkata?
A 3-day trip from Kolkata costs around ₹7,000-9,000 per person for budget travellers, ₹13,000-16,000 per person for mid-range stays, and ₹30,000+ per person for luxury heritage hotels. Train return tickets between Kolkata (Sealdah/Howrah) and NJP cost ₹500-2,500 depending on class.
Is Darjeeling safe for solo female travellers?
Yes, Darjeeling is one of the safest hill stations in India for solo female travellers. The locals are friendly and respectful, the town has reliable mobile and internet connectivity, and many hostels and homestays specifically cater to solo women. We still recommend basic precautions: avoid deserted areas after 9 PM, inform your homestay if you're heading out for an early Tiger Hill trip, and use registered shared jeeps rather than unmarked vehicles.
Is the Darjeeling toy train worth it?
The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway joyride is absolutely worth it for first-time visitors. The 2-hour Darjeeling-Ghoom-Darjeeling ride passes through Batasia Loop, climbs to the highest railway station in India (Ghoom at 7,407 ft), and runs on a UNESCO World Heritage track. Book first-class tickets through IRCTC at least a week in advance during peak season.
Can I see Mount Everest from Darjeeling?
On extremely clear mornings between November and February, you can see Mount Everest from Tiger Hill - it appears as a tiny peak on the far horizon to the west of Kanchenjunga. October-November and March give the highest success rate for spotting Everest.
What is special about Darjeeling tea?
Darjeeling tea is a Geographical Indication tagged product, often called "the champagne of teas". Grown only on the slopes of the Darjeeling region between 600 and 2,000 metres, it has a distinct muscatel flavour and floral aroma. The first-flush variety, harvested in March-April, is the most expensive and is exported globally.
Is Darjeeling good for honeymoon?
Darjeeling is one of the most popular honeymoon destinations in eastern India. Heritage hotels like Windamere, Mayfair and Glenburn Tea Estate offer fireplaces, candlelit dinners and Kanchenjunga-facing suites. Plan for 4 to 5 days. Pair with Gangtok for a 7-day Darjeeling-Sikkim honeymoon circuit.
Do I need a permit to visit Darjeeling?
Indian nationals do not need any permit to visit Darjeeling. Foreign tourists do not require a special permit for Darjeeling town, but if you plan to trek to Singalila National Park or Sandakphu, you'll need a Singalila Trekking Permit from the Forest Department - usually arranged by your trek operator.
